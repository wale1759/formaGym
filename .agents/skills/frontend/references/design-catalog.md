# Product Design Catalog

Use this small, original catalog to find a direction that fits the product. Match by product, user, and task—not by trend. Read only the best one or two entries for a request. These are starting points for discussion, not a rigid style recipe or a claim of user research.

When the direction is unclear, turn suitable matches into two or three brief options and recommend one. Explain the choice in plain language. Ask the user to choose or adjust it before building a new project or substantial feature.

## Learning and practice

**Tags:** education, learning, course, practice, quiz, student, progress

- **Useful structure:** show the learner where they are, the next small action, and how to continue. Keep lesson content focused; place progress and feedback close to the activity.
- **Visual direction:** clear and encouraging. Use a calm base with a small number of lively accents, or a more playful direction when the audience and task support it.
- **Type and color roles:** prioritize readable lesson text; give primary actions, progress, correct answers, and errors distinct roles. Never rely on color alone to communicate an answer.
- **Interaction:** give immediate, understandable feedback; preserve progress; make reminders and celebrations supportive and easy to dismiss.
- **Avoid:** turning every screen into a game, distracting animation during learning, or using badges and streaks as a substitute for useful feedback.
- **Page references:** Duolingo home; Khan Academy home.

## Money, payments, and financial tools

**Tags:** finance, fintech, payment, banking, budget, invoice, pricing, trust

- **Useful structure:** keep balances, prices, fees, and next steps easy to scan. Explain totals and consequences before a user confirms a payment or financial action.
- **Visual direction:** organized and trustworthy. Use restrained decoration and clear number hierarchy; make status colors consistent and pair them with labels.
- **Type and color roles:** ensure figures and terms are readable; distinguish primary actions, supporting information, warnings, and confirmed states.
- **Interaction:** show review and confirmation for consequential actions; explain errors and recovery steps without exposing sensitive data.
- **Avoid:** hidden fees, unexplained status colors, false urgency, and decorative effects that weaken trust.
- **Page references:** Stripe pricing.

## Marketplace, travel, and booking

**Tags:** marketplace, travel, booking, rental, search, listing, filters, availability

- **Useful structure:** make search inputs and important filters easy to find; show the information people need to compare options, such as price, availability, location, and key attributes.
- **Visual direction:** let meaningful images help users compare, while keeping search controls and details legible. Choose card or list layouts based on the amount of comparison information.
- **Type and color roles:** make price, dates, availability, and primary actions distinct. Keep labels visible and consistent across results and details.
- **Interaction:** preserve search criteria while browsing; make filters removable and results understandable when none match.
- **Avoid:** hiding total cost, making image galleries the only source of information, or using filters that are difficult to discover or clear.
- **Page references:** Airbnb search results.

## Developer tools and work management

**Tags:** developer tool, dashboard, project management, issues, tasks, analytics, productivity

- **Useful structure:** show current work, status, and next actions first. Use tables, boards, timelines, and charts only when they help the user's task; allow details to be opened without losing context.
- **Visual direction:** focused and information-dense when needed, with strong hierarchy and deliberate whitespace. Support light or dark themes when the product requires them.
- **Type and color roles:** prioritize readable labels, dates, and data; make status and priority consistent and never color-only.
- **Interaction:** provide useful filters and sorting, keyboard access, clear empty states, and visible save or update feedback.
- **Avoid:** filling every space with cards, charts without a decision purpose, and tiny low-contrast metadata.
- **Page references:** Linear features; GitHub Issues.

## Online stores and commerce

**Tags:** ecommerce, online store, shop, product page, checkout, inventory, retail

- **Useful structure:** help users discover products, compare options, understand delivery and returns, and complete checkout with minimal surprise.
- **Visual direction:** use product photography and a clear type hierarchy; keep branding distinctive while making product details and actions easy to scan.
- **Type and color roles:** distinguish product name, price, options, availability, delivery information, and the purchase action.
- **Interaction:** make variants, cart updates, validation, and checkout status clear on mobile and desktop.
- **Avoid:** false scarcity, surprise costs late in checkout, autoplaying product media, or making essential details hard to find.
- **Page references:** Shopify Editions.

## Public services and multi-step forms

**Tags:** public service, government, form, application, eligibility, account, questions

- **Useful structure:** focus each step on a clear task; explain why information is needed; show progress when it helps; provide a review and confirmation step for longer applications.
- **Visual direction:** prioritize clarity, legibility, and predictable navigation over decoration.
- **Type and color roles:** use plain labels, helpful hints, clear error messages, and consistent focus and status cues.
- **Interaction:** preserve entered answers where practical; move focus to errors accessibly; make back, edit, and recovery paths clear.
- **Avoid:** asking for unnecessary personal data, vague field labels, or resetting a form after an error.
- **Page references:** GOV.UK Design System patterns.

## Product feature and marketing pages

**Tags:** SaaS, landing page, product page, feature page, launch, marketing, pricing

- **Useful structure:** state what the product does and who it helps; show a small number of meaningful benefits, evidence, and a clear next action.
- **Visual direction:** choose one product-specific visual idea and use it consistently. Use screenshots or illustrations only when they clarify the product.
- **Type and color roles:** create a clear heading, body, supporting detail, and action hierarchy; preserve readable contrast.
- **Interaction:** use navigation and calls to action that match the user's stage; make pricing and important limitations easy to find when relevant.
- **Avoid:** generic hero copy, repeated feature-card grids, decorative dashboards that do not represent the product, and unsupported claims.
- **Page references:** Linear features; Stripe pricing; Shopify Editions.
